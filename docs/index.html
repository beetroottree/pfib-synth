pfib-synth

A plasma focused-ion-beam microscope (pFIB-SEM) images a material by destroying it carefully: an ion beam shaves off a slice thinner than a pixel, an electron beam photographs the fresh face, and this repeats hundreds of times. Stacking the photographs gives a 3-D image of the material. In the real data nobody knows which pixels are truly pore and which are solid, because the electron beam also sees down into open pores. Here the material is built first, so every pixel of every slice has an exact answer.

Cap (top)A protective layer deposited before milling. Smooth, featureless, and not scored.
Porous layer (middle)The material being measured. Bright is solid; dark is pore. Grey inside a pore is the wall or floor below the cut, seen through the opening.
Support (bottom)The substrate the layer sits on, with its own large cavities. Not scored.
Exact truthBlack pore, white solid, grey outside the layer. It comes from the 3-D model, not from anyone drawing it.
